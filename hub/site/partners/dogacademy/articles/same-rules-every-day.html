Keep the same rules every day

Dog Academy staff Human-made

Sponsored by Dog Academy

Inspired by widely shared training tips from Cesar Millan.

Official site of Cesar Millan

Not affiliated with or endorsed by Cesar Millan.

Dogs live with whatever the house actually does, not with the rules people mean to have. If the sofa is allowed on weeknights and forbidden when guests arrive, the rule is not a rule. This practice asks you to choose two boundaries and keep them boringly consistent for seven days. It will not make a dog perfectly behaved, and it is not a plan for aggression. If a rule makes you or the dog unsafe, drop it and talk with a qualified trainer or a veterinarian.

Write the two rules on a note you will see. Good examples are: paws stay on the floor when people enter, and the dog waits until the bowl is set down before eating. Bad first rules are vague, such as be good, or huge, such as never bark. You want rules a visitor could follow after one sentence of instruction.

Step one. Tell every person in the home the two rules in the same words. Agree what you will do when the rule is broken. A simple version: pause the activity, reset, and try again once. Agree what you will not do: no shouting contest, no extra affection while the dog is mid jump, and no changing the rule because you are tired. If someone cannot follow it, pick a different rule.

Step two. Practice each rule at the moment it matters, not in a lecture. For the door, have one person step outside and knock. The people inside keep the greeting quiet until paws are down, or until you have the dog on a leash behind you. Then the person enters. One knock is one rep. Do three reps. For the bowl, hold the bowl, set it down only when the dog is not jumping into your hands, and pick it up again if the dog crowds you. Reset once.

Step three. For the rest of the week, do not add a third rule. When you slip, note it without drama and return to the same standard at the next chance. Consistency is the practice. Perfection is not the assignment. A house that keeps two rules is easier to understand than a house that announces ten and keeps none.

At the end of the week, keep the rules that the whole home actually held, and retire the ones that depended on one person's mood. This is housekeeping, not a full plan. Dogs with a bite history, dogs who guard food in a way that scares you, and dogs who may be in pain need a qualified trainer or a veterinarian, not a stricter slogan.
